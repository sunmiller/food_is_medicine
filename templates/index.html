Study Nature

I enjoy studying nature because it helps me understand how living things and the environment are connected. I have cared for pet snakes, maintained a terrarium, and even raised ladybugs, which taught me about animal behavior and ecosystems. I also presented a science exhibition project called "Cows Pollute," where I explained how cows produce methane gas, a greenhouse gas that contributes to climate change. My project showed that adding seaweed to a cow's diet can help reduce methane emissions. I enjoy learning from soil, insects, plants, microbes, water, and air because they all play an important role in keeping our planet healthy. These experiences have inspired me to keep exploring nature and learning more about environmental science.

Restore Habitats

I believe that healthy habitats are essential for protecting wildlife and maintaining a healthy environment. I have volunteered at many park cleanups, where I helped remove litter and restore natural areas for people and animals to enjoy. I have also studied water quality in streams to better understand how pollution affects aquatic ecosystems and the plants and animals that depend on clean water. In addition, I have learned about organic soil and how healthy soil supports plant growth, insects, and other living organisms. I support native plants, pollinators, and clean green spaces because they help create balanced ecosystems and protect biodiversity. Through these experiences, I have learned that even small actions can make a meaningful difference in restoring and preserving natural habitats.

My Journey Studying Diabetes

My interest in diabetes began with a simple question: Can food help people live healthier lives? That question led me to spend hundreds of hours studying how different foods affect blood sugar and overall metabolic health.

I have researched topics such as the glycemic index, glycemic load, carbohydrates, fiber, healthy fats, protein, and the role each plays in managing diabetes. I regularly read scientific studies, review nutrition databases, and compare evidence from trusted medical organizations to better understand how food influences the body.

I created Eat for Healing to organize that knowledge into a simple tool that anyone can use. My goal is to help people discover foods that support healthier blood sugar levels and make informed nutrition decisions based on science-not myths or fad diets.

This website is more than a food search engine. It represents my ongoing commitment to learning about diabetes, nutrition, and preventive health. As I continue my research, I will keep improving the information and tools available here so others can benefit from what I learn.

Knowledge is powerful. The right food choices can make a meaningful difference, and every healthy meal is a step toward better health.
